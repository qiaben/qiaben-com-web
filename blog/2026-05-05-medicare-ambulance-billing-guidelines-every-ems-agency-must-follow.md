## Medicare Part B and Ambulance Coverage

### How Medicare Part B Covers Ambulance Transport

Medicare ambulance billing guidelines fall under Medicare Part B, which covers medically necessary ambulance transportation when other means of transport would be contraindicated. Medicare Part B covers both emergency and non-emergency ambulance transports, but each comes with specific documentation requirements.

For emergency transports, the sudden onset of a medical condition requiring immediate treatment is generally sufficient to establish medical necessity. For non-emergency transports, Medicare ambulance billing guidelines require specific documentation showing that:

- The patient's condition required ambulance transport
- The patient was bed-confined or could not be transported safely by other means
- The transport was to or from a Medicare-covered service

### Medicare Enrollment Requirements

Before billing Medicare for ambulance services, your agency must be enrolled as a Medicare supplier. Medicare enrollment requires:

- Completion of CMS Form 855B (Medicare Enrollment Application for Clinics/Group Practices)
- Active National Provider Identifier (NPI)
- State licensure for ambulance services
- Meeting all applicable Medicare Conditions of Participation

## Medical Necessity Documentation Requirements

### What CMS Requires for Every Transport

Medical necessity documentation is the most scrutinized aspect of Medicare ambulance billing guidelines. CMS requires that every ambulance claim be supported by documentation that establishes the medical necessity of the transport. Required documentation includes:

1. The patient's medical condition at the time of transport
2. Why the patient required ambulance transport specifically
3. Why other means of transport were not appropriate
4. The patient's bed-confined status for non-emergency transports (if applicable)
5. The attending physician's certification of medical necessity for scheduled non-emergency transports (Physician Certification Statement or PCS)

Incomplete or vague medical necessity documentation is the leading cause of Medicare claim denials and audit findings for EMS agencies.

### The Physician Certification Statement

For non-emergency, repetitive transports (such as dialysis runs), Medicare ambulance billing guidelines require a Physician Certification Statement (PCS) signed by the treating physician. The PCS must:

- Be signed before or shortly after the transport
- Contain the physician's NPI
- Specify why the patient requires ambulance transport
- Cover no more than 60 days of transport

## ALS vs. BLS Billing Under Medicare

### Understanding Level-of-Service Distinctions

One of the most important and frequently audited aspects of Medicare ambulance billing guidelines is the distinction between ALS (Advanced Life Support) and BLS (Basic Life Support) billing. Medicare pays significantly more for ALS transports, which is why they receive close scrutiny.

Under Medicare ambulance billing guidelines:

- **BLS (A0429)**: Transport by an ambulance staffed with at least one EMT-Basic
- **ALS Level 1 (A0427)**: Transport with ALS assessment by a paramedic or ALS intervention
- **ALS Level 2 (A0433)**: Transport requiring at least three ALS interventions
- **Specialty Care Transport (A0434)**: Transport requiring a critical care level provider

The level billed must match the documented interventions and crew certifications. Billing ALS when documentation supports only BLS is a compliance violation under Medicare ambulance billing guidelines.

  

## Medicare Fee Schedule and Reimbursement Rates

### How the Medicare Ambulance Fee Schedule Works

CMS updates the Medicare fee schedule for ambulance services annually. Reimbursement rates are based on:

- Level of service (BLS, ALS1, ALS2, SCT)
- Base rate by urban, rural, or super-rural geographic designation
- Loaded mileage rate
- Origin and destination modifiers

Understanding the Medicare fee schedule helps agencies ensure they're billing correctly and identifying any underpayments from Medicare. Qiaben monitors Medicare fee schedule updates and ensures your billing always reflects current allowable rates.

### Prior Authorization Waiver Program

CMS implemented a Medicare prior authorization model for non-emergency repetitive ambulance transports in select states. Under this program, providers must obtain prior authorization before billing Medicare for certain transport types. The prior authorization waiver program affects non-emergency scheduled transports and requires advance approval to receive payment.
