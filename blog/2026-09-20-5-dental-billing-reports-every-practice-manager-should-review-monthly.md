Running a dental practice involves more than treating patients and scheduling appointments. Practice managers also need to understand how the business is performing financially. Billing reports provide useful information about payments, unpaid claims, outstanding balances, and collection problems.

Reviewing these reports every month helps managers identify issues early and make better decisions about the practice's revenue. Instead of waiting until payments are delayed or outstanding balances become too large, they can use the information to improve daily billing operations.

Here are five important dental billing reports every practice manager should review monthly.

![5 Dental Billing Reports Every Practice Manager Should Review Monthly](/img/uploads/2026/09/5-dental-billing-reports-every-practice-manager-should-review-monthly.png)

## 1. Accounts Receivable (A/R) Aging Report

The A/R aging report shows how much money the practice is waiting to collect from insurance companies and patients. It separates outstanding balances into different time periods, usually 0-30, 31-60, 61-90, and over 90 days.

This report helps managers understand how long payments have remained unpaid. A large balance in the older categories may indicate delayed claim follow-ups, unresolved denials, or patient payment issues.

Reviewing this report monthly helps practices prioritize older balances and maintain a more consistent collection process.

## 2. Claim Submission and Rejection Report

This report shows how many claims were submitted during the month and how many were accepted or rejected by the clearinghouse or payer.

A high rejection rate may point to problems such as incorrect patient information, missing details, coding errors, or incomplete documentation. These issues can create additional work because claims may need to be corrected and submitted again.

Practice managers should look for repeated rejection reasons instead of treating every rejected claim as an isolated issue. If the same error appears regularly, the team may need better verification procedures or additional staff training.

A monthly review also helps managers understand whether claim preparation and submission processes are improving.

## 3. Denial Report

A denial report helps managers understand why insurance companies are not paying certain claims. Common reasons include eligibility problems, missing documentation, coding mistakes, authorization issues, and filing deadlines.

The report should show the number of denied claims, the amounts involved, and the most frequent denial reasons. This information helps the practice identify where revenue is being delayed or lost.

The goal is not just to count denials but to understand their causes and reduce repeat errors.

## 4. Payment and Collection Report

The payment and collection report shows how much money the practice actually received during the month. It may include insurance payments, patient payments, adjustments, and outstanding amounts.

Comparing collections with the amount billed gives managers a clearer picture of how effectively the practice is collecting revenue.

For example, if the practice provides a high volume of services but collections remain low, managers may need to investigate delayed insurance payments, unposted payments, or patient balances that have not been followed up.

This report also helps the team understand whether collection efforts are keeping pace with the services provided.

## 5. Patient Balance Report

After the payer processes a claim, some charges may become the patient's responsibility. The patient balance report shows how much patients still owe and how long those balances have remained unpaid.

Practice managers should review this report to identify accounts that need statements, payment reminders, or follow-up communication. They should also check whether insurance payments and adjustments have been posted correctly before contacting patients.

Clear communication is important. Patients should understand what their insurance covers, why a balance remains, and what payment options are available. A consistent process can help practices manage outstanding patient balances without creating unnecessary confusion.

## How to Use These Reports Effectively

Preparing reports is only useful when the practice takes action based on the information. Managers should review the same key metrics every month and compare them with previous months to identify changes.

If claim rejections increase, the team can review its submission process. If older A/R balances grow, staff can prioritize follow-ups. If patient balances remain unpaid, the practice can improve its billing communication.

It is also helpful to assign responsibility for each issue. When team members know who handles claim corrections, payment posting, and patient follow-ups, fewer accounts are likely to be overlooked.

A reliable dental billing process depends on accurate information, regular monitoring, and timely action. Managers should use reports to identify problems and improve the workflow rather than simply storing monthly figures.

## Conclusion

The A/R aging, claim submission, denial, payment and collection, and patient balance reports give dental practice managers a useful overview of their financial performance.

Reviewing these reports monthly helps identify delayed payments, recurring errors, and collection gaps before they become bigger problems. With consistent monitoring and effective dental billing services, practices can improve claim management, keep outstanding balances under control, and support a healthier revenue cycle.
