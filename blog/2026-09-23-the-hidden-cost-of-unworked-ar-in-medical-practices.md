Unworked accounts receivable (A/R) can quietly affect a medical practice's financial performance. When unpaid claims are not reviewed or followed up on, payments may be delayed, denials may go unresolved, and staff may spend more time correcting old billing issues. These problems can reduce cash flow and make it harder for a practice to manage its daily expenses.

A/R represents money a practice expects to receive for the medical services it has provided. Not every unpaid claim is a problem, because insurance companies need time to process claims. However, when claims remain unpaid without regular follow-up, the practice may lose opportunities to collect payments on time.

Understanding the hidden costs of unworked A/R helps practices improve their billing process and maintain more predictable revenue.

![The Hidden Cost of Unworked A/R](/img/uploads/2026/09/the-hidden-cost-of-unworked-ar.png)

## 1. Delayed Payments Can Affect Cash Flow

Medical practices depend on regular payments to cover staff salaries, rent, medical supplies, equipment, and other operating expenses. When claims remain unworked for weeks or months, the expected money may not arrive when it is needed.

This can create financial pressure even when the practice is providing services and generating claims consistently. The practice may need to delay purchases, adjust budgets, or spend additional time checking payment statuses.

Regular A/R reviews help staff identify claims that need attention before they become significantly overdue. Following up with payers, correcting errors, and submitting requested documents promptly can help reduce unnecessary payment delays.

## 2. Old Claims Become Harder to Resolve

The longer a claim remains unpaid, the more difficult it may become to resolve. Staff may need to retrieve older medical records, review previous payer communications, or determine why an earlier follow-up was not completed.

Some claims may also approach payer deadlines for corrections or appeals. If the practice misses the applicable deadline, it may lose the opportunity to receive payment. The specific rules depend on the payer, claim type, and applicable agreement.

To avoid this situation, practices should monitor unpaid claims by age and prioritize those that need immediate action. Claims with approaching deadlines, unresolved denials, or missing documentation should not remain in the queue without an assigned next step.

## 3. Unworked A/R Increases Administrative Work

Leaving claims unresolved does not eliminate the work involved. Instead, it often moves the work to a later date, when the claim may require more investigation.

For example, a billing employee may need to contact an insurance company, check the original claim, review an explanation of benefits, and compare the information with the patient's record. If the reason for the delay was never documented, another employee may need to repeat the same investigation.

This repeated work reduces the time available for current claims and other important billing activities. A clear tracking system can help prevent duplication by recording the claim status, reason for delay, previous action, responsible staff member, and next follow-up date.

## 4. Denials May Remain Unnoticed

Some unpaid claims are denied, but a denial can go unresolved when payer responses are not reviewed consistently. If the practice does not identify the reason for denial, it may miss the chance to correct the claim or submit an appropriate appeal.

Common reasons include incorrect patient information, eligibility problems, coding errors, missing documentation, and authorization issues. Each denial should be reviewed individually because the required correction depends on the payer's explanation.

Practices should also look for repeated denial patterns. If many claims are denied for the same reason, the issue may be related to registration, documentation, coding, or claim submission procedures. Fixing the underlying cause can help prevent similar problems in future billing cycles.

## 5. Poor A/R Tracking Hides Revenue Problems

A total A/R balance does not always show where a practice is losing time or facing collection difficulties. Without further analysis, staff may not know whether claims are waiting for payer processing, missing documents, corrections, or follow-up.

Reviewing A/R by aging category, payer, denial reason, and claim status provides a clearer picture. For example, a growing number of older claims with one payer may indicate a recurring processing or follow-up issue. A high number of claims awaiting documentation may point to a gap in the internal workflow.

These patterns help practice managers decide where to focus their attention. The goal is not simply to reduce the outstanding balance, but to understand why claims remain unpaid and take appropriate action.

## 6. Improve the A/R Follow-Up Process

A consistent follow-up routine helps ensure that outstanding claims receive attention. Staff should review unpaid claims regularly, verify their current status, and document the next action required.

A practical process includes checking payer responses, correcting rejected claims, submitting requested information, and appealing denials when appropriate. Each claim should have a clear owner and a follow-up date so that responsibility does not become unclear.

Medical practices can also review their [medical billing services](/medical-billing-service) workflow to identify gaps in claim submission, payment posting, denial management, and A/R follow-up. Regular monitoring helps teams identify issues early and make improvements based on actual claim trends.

## 7. Review Reimbursement Accuracy

Not all payment problems result from missed follow-ups. Some claims may be paid incorrectly because of coding issues, incomplete documentation, or reimbursement discrepancies. These problems can affect the amount a practice receives and may require additional review.

This is particularly important for hospitals working with Diagnosis-Related Group (DRG) payments. Accurate clinical documentation and appropriate coding help support correct reimbursement under the applicable payment rules.

## 8. Measure Progress With Regular A/R Reviews

Practices should track whether their follow-up efforts are improving results. Useful measures include the age of outstanding balances, the number of unresolved denials, common reasons for claim delays, and the time taken to complete follow-up actions.

These measures should be reviewed consistently rather than only when cash flow becomes a concern. If older balances continue to increase, the practice may need to adjust staffing, improve its tracking process, or address recurring claim errors.

It is also important to set realistic expectations. Some claims require additional payer review, and not every outstanding balance can be collected. Regular analysis helps teams distinguish normal processing delays from problems that need corrective action.

## Final Thoughts

Unworked A/R can create hidden costs through delayed cash flow, missed deadlines, unresolved denials, and repeated administrative work. These problems can become harder to manage when claims are not tracked and assigned for follow-up.

Medical practices can improve A/R management by reviewing outstanding claims regularly, prioritizing urgent cases, identifying recurring errors, and documenting every follow-up action. A consistent process helps the team understand where payments are delayed and take practical steps to resolve the underlying issues.
