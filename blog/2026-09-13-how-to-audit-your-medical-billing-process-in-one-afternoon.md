A medical billing audit does not always require several days of work. With a focused approach, a healthcare practice can review its billing process in one afternoon and identify common errors, documentation gaps, delayed claims, and areas that need improvement.

The goal is not to examine every claim in the system. Instead, select a small but representative sample and look for patterns. This gives your team a practical view of what may be going wrong without interrupting the normal workflow.

![Audit Your Medical Billing in One Afternoon](/img/uploads/2026/09/audit-your-medical-billing-in-one-afternoon.png)

## Start With a Small Sample of Claims

Begin by selecting a group of recent claims. Include different types of claims rather than reviewing only successfully paid ones.

Your sample can include paid claims, denied claims, rejected claims, and claims that are still pending. If possible, choose claims from different providers, payers, or service types.

A small sample can often reveal repeated problems. If the same type of error appears across several claims, it may indicate a process issue that needs attention.

## Check Patient and Insurance Information

Review the basic information on each selected claim. Compare the claim with the patient's records and check details such as the patient's name, date of birth, insurance information, member ID, provider information, and date of service.

Even a small registration or eligibility error can lead to a rejected or delayed claim. This part of the audit can help determine whether problems are beginning during patient registration or insurance verification.

## Review Coding and Documentation

Next, compare the services billed with the documentation in the patient's record.

Ask:

- Does the documentation support the service billed?
- Are the diagnosis and procedure codes appropriate for the documented service?
- Are required details present?
- Do the dates and provider information match?
- Is any important documentation missing?

The purpose is to make sure the claim tells the same story as the medical record. A claim may look complete on the billing side but still have a problem if the documentation does not support the services reported.

## Examine Denials and Rejections

A useful audit should look beyond the number of denied claims. Review the reason behind each denial or rejection.

Group the problems into categories such as eligibility, coding, missing information, authorization, documentation, duplicate claims, or filing issues.

Then look for repeated patterns. If several claims were denied for the same reason, the problem may be related to the billing workflow rather than individual claims.

For practices that use external medical billing services, this review can also help identify which parts of the billing process need better communication, monitoring, or follow-up.

## Review Outstanding Accounts

Spend some time checking unpaid claims and aging accounts receivable. Focus on claims that have been outstanding for longer than expected.

Determine whether each claim is still processing, has been denied, requires additional information, or needs follow-up with the payer.

The objective is not simply to find old balances. It is to understand why those balances remain unpaid and whether similar delays are happening repeatedly.

## Look at the Entire Billing Workflow

After reviewing individual claims, step back and examine the complete process. Ask where errors are most frequently entering the process.

For example, repeated eligibility errors may point to a verification problem. Frequent coding issues may indicate a need for additional review or training. Delayed follow-up may suggest that unpaid claims are not being monitored consistently.

Automation can also support several parts of this workflow by helping with repetitive checks, claim tracking, and billing information. However, technology should support the billing team rather than replace careful review of complex cases.

## Create an Action List

Before finishing the audit, write down the issues you discovered. Separate them into three simple categories:

- **Fix now:** Problems that may affect current claims or payments.
- **Fix soon:** Repeated workflow issues that require a process change.
- **Monitor:** Minor issues that should be checked during future audits.

This makes the audit more useful because your team leaves the review with clear next steps rather than just a list of problems.

## A Simple Afternoon Audit Schedule

You can divide the review into four stages:

- **First 30 minutes:** Select claims and verify patient and insurance information.
- **Next 45 minutes:** Review coding and supporting documentation.
- **Next 30 minutes:** Examine denials, rejections, and outstanding accounts.
- **Final 15 minutes:** Identify repeated problems and create an action list.

The timing can be adjusted depending on the size of the practice and the number of claims being reviewed.

## Final Thoughts

A medical billing audit does not have to be complicated or time-consuming. A focused afternoon can help uncover claim errors, documentation gaps, denial patterns, and workflow issues.

The most important part is what happens after the audit. If the same problem appears repeatedly, address the underlying process instead of correcting each claim individually. Regular, focused reviews can help a practice maintain a more accurate and organized billing workflow.
