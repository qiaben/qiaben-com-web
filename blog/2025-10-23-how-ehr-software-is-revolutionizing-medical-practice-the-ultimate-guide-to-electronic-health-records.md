## The ABCs: EHR vs. EMR

### What is an EHR?

- An [Electronic Health Record (EHR)](https://qiaben.com/how-dental-narratives-enhance-patient-experience/) is an comprehensive, digital interpretation of a case’s medical history.
- EHRs are designed to be shared across health care organizations, and several providers can have access to one patient's information.
- Core elements of data include clinical notes, lab results, medication lists, medical histories, and patient demographics.

### What is an EMR?

- An [Electronic Medical Record (EMR)](https://qiaben.com/customer-cabinet/) is another electronic patient record but typically confined to the office of a single doctor.
- EMRs are mainly utilized to help clinicians see patient data longitudinally but are less transportable than EHRs.
- EMR systems are less interested in reporting office internal operations and care.

Learn more about the differences between [EHR vs EMR](https://www.healthit.gov/faq/what-electronic-medical-record-emr)

### EHR vs EMR: What's the Difference?

Being aware of the differences helps avoid medical practices getting the wrong software. Here is a side-by-side summary:

#### Interoperability

- EHR: Created to share with multiple health care providers and systems.
- EMR: Destination is a single practice; sharing is very restricted.

#### Data Scope

- EHR: Entire scope; lab results, images, meds, allergy data, and visit summary.
- EMR: Diagnosis and treatment of an individual office.

#### Patient Engagement

- EHR: Patient-consumable support for viewing, scheduling, and contacting providers.
- EMR: Patient-consumable function is minimal.

#### Cost & Implementation

- EHR: More costly upfront but long-term benefits with interoperability and productivity.
- EMR: Lower cost, less challenging to implement, but less scalable.

### FAQs on EHR Software

#### 1\. What is the difference between EHR and EMR?

EMR is just one practice in isolation; EHR is meant to be shared by a multitude of providers and health systems.

#### 2\. How can the effectiveness of dental practice be optimized using EHR software?

By automating scheduling, paperwork, and billing, prevent minimization of errors and staff time.

#### 3\. Are the EHR systems HIPAA compliant?

Good EHRs are typically [HIPAA compliant](https://qiaben.com/customer-cabinet/), allowing for secure storage and sharing of [patient records.](https://qiaben.com/how-dental-narratives-enhance-patient-experience/)

#### 4\. Can patient records be accessed by patients through EHR?

Yes, the patient portal offers patients the ability to view records, request appointments, and reach providers.

#### 5\. What are the primary benefits of the move from EMR to EHR?

More interoperability, more patient engagement, more analytics, and enhanced workflow efficiency.

#### 6\. Is EHR software costly for small practices?

There may be an enormous initial cost, but overall effectiveness, fewer errors, and improved patient care long term.
