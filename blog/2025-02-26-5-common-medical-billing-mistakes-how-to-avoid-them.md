### 1.Inaccurate Patient Demographics

Something as simple as a misspelled name, incorrect date of birth, or outdated insurance ID can derail a claim. According to MGMA, up to 25% of denials stem from invalid patient data.

**How to Avoid It:**

- Train front-desk staff to double-check patient information at scheduling and check-in.
    
- Use real-time insurance verification tools to confirm coverage before appointments.
    
- Standardize data entry workflows to minimize typos and mismatched records.
    

### 2\. Missing Pre-Authorizations

Many procedures, lab tests, and imaging services require pre-authorization from insurance providers. Failing to secure approval in advance almost guarantees claim denials and revenue write-offs.

**How to Avoid It:**

- Assign a dedicated staff member or team for pre-certifications.
    
- Use billing software that tracks prior authorization requests and follow-ups.
    
- Educate clinical staff on which procedures require prior approval to reduce errors at the source.
    

### 3\. Incorrect or Outdated Medical Codes

Using the wrong CPT, HCPCS, or ICD-10 codes is one of the most frequent **medical billing errors**. Lack of specificity or outdated [**medical codes**](https://www.aapc.com/codes/cpt-codes-range/?srsltid=AfmBOop8BTepx7n2q7XwDremtyPNLm4hil6GWqLZxob5z11nQwZmi6lZ) often results in claim rejections or reduced reimbursements.

**How to Avoid It:**

- Keep billing staff updated on annual coding changes from CMS and AMA.
    
- Invest in coding education and certifications.
    
- Leverage coding software and claim scrubbing tools to validate codes before submission.
    

### 4\. Duplicate Billing

Submitting multiple claims for the same service—whether accidental or due to unbundling errors—can trigger denials and even compliance audits.

**How to Avoid It:**

- Implement claim scrubbing systems that detect duplicate entries.
    
- Train billing staff on proper bundling practices.
    
- Conduct routine internal audits to flag duplicate or suspicious claim activity.

### 5\. Timely Filing Errors

Every payer has strict filing deadlines, usually within 90–180 days of service. Late submissions nearly always result in denials, leaving providers with no chance of reimbursement.

**How to Avoid It:**

- Set automated reminders in your billing system for claim deadlines.
    
- Establish a consistent internal workflow for claim submission within days of service.
    
- Track and report filing timelines regularly to ensure compliance.
