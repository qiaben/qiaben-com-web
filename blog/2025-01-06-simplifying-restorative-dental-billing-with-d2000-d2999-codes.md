## 3\. Common Coding Errors to Avoid

Many errors repeat across practices. To prevent denials:

- **Do not overuse D2999** - It triggers audits when used without justification.
    
- **Avoid missing paired codes** - Crowns often require a supporting core buildup.
    
- **Reduce unnecessary recement codes (D2910)** - Document each case thoroughly.
    
- **Check frequency limits** - Many fillings and crowns have time-based restrictions.
    
- **Attach complete records** - Include radiographs, prep notes, and lab documentation.
    

## 4\. Correct Use of D2999 (“By Report”)

D2999 should be used only when no accurate code exists.  
To improve approval chances:

Use D2999 when:

- The procedure is new or has no defined CDT code.
    
- Materials or techniques are unique or custom.
    

Required Documentation:

- A clear description of the service.
    
- Clinical justification with supporting evidence.
    
- Provider details, date, and narrative.
    

Red Flags:

- Frequent use of D2999
    
- Missing narrative or attachments

## 5\. Frequency of Coverage & Plan Limitations 

Repair procedures routinely carry frequency limitations:

- D2330 (fillings): 2–5 years based on plan.
- D2740 (crowns): Generally covered every 5–10 years.
- D2910 (recement): Typically every 2 years.
- D2999: Weighed under "usual and customary" and routinely requires justification.

Tip: Check frequency prior to treatment with real-time payer resources and update patient intake forms with their coverage information.

## 6\. Documentation & Submission Best Practices

Accurate documentation greatly improves claim acceptance. Follow these steps:

- **Use narrative templates** to keep submissions consistent.
    
- **Attach all necessary documents** including X-rays, prep notes, and lab slips.
    
- **Match chart notes to billing codes** to avoid discrepancies.
    
- **Avoid upcoding**-only bill for the exact procedure performed.
    
- **Scrub claims** with software tools to catch errors before submission.

## 14\. SEO-Friendly FAQs

#### 1\. When should I use D2999?

Use it only when no defined CDT code fits. Provide a complete narrative and supporting documentation.

#### 2\. Are crown photos and lab slips required?

Yes. These help reduce audits and support the necessity of D2740.

#### 3\. How often can a crown be re-cemented?

Most plans allow this once every two years.

#### 4\. What if a crown payment posts partially?

Review coverage first and appeal the claim if needed.

#### 5\. Can fillings be replaced before 2 years?

Yes, but you must provide radiographic proof of new decay or failure.
